## 3. Episode structure (the beat map)

Target **55–65 s**, with the **movie playing alone for 40–50% of it** and the **voiceover at 90–115 words total.**

```
PLAY   2–4 s   movie with its own audio. The scene sets itself up. No narration.
PAUSE  click SFX + freeze. HOOK REACTION (≤ 12 words): an interjection or a deadpan verdict.
PLAY   3–8 s   the movie escalates; let it breathe
PAUSE  click. SETUP + ONE NUMBER (≤ 2 sentences) → SARCASTIC PUNCH (1 sentence)
PLAY   3–6 s
PAUSE  click. ONE COMPARISON or the "it gets worse" twist → PUNCH
PLAY   3–8 s   the payoff moment of the scene
PAUSE  click. VERDICT + CLOSER JOKE (+ optional self-deprecating CTA)
(optional) PLAY 1–3 s  end on the action
```

Rules:
- **Every explanation sentence earns a reaction line right after it.** The ratio of jokes/reactions to facts is at least 1:1.
- **No more than ~12 s of narration in a row.** Then the movie plays again.
- **One number per pause, max.** Round it, and say it like a person ("about fifty G's", not "50.7 m/s²").
- **Pauses land on a frozen frame that is itself funny**: a mid-impact face, the moment before disaster.
- The hook is a **reaction**, never a fact. ✅ "Nope. Absolutely not." ✅ "Wait, they're doing WHAT?" ✅ "Cooper is about to dock with a ship spinning like a washing machine. Okay." ❌ "The Endurance is spinning at 68 RPM."

## 4. Voice and humor toolbox (sarcasm sources)

- **Mock the character's decision-making:** "Cooper looked at a spinning space station and thought, yeah, I can parallel park that."
- **Understatement:** "Slightly concerning." / "Minor issue." (about something lethal)
- **Fake respect:** "Respect. Your spine, however, does not respect you."
- **Running gags:** "dead again" style, e.g. "And that's another fatality for the body-count spreadsheet."
- **Imagined third parties:** the insurance company, the person who built it, HR, his chiropractor.
- **Rhetorical double-take:** "Wait. Wait wait wait." / "Hold on."
- **Deadpan verdicts:** "Physics: zero. Plot armor: undefeated."
- **Honest-credit flips:** "Okay, credit where it's due…" (builds trust, then undercut it).
- Keep it PG-13, punch at the movie and the characters, never at the viewers or real people.

## 6. Editing changes the template needs (before the next render)

- **Freeze-frame PAUSE beats**: the movie stops on a frame while the VO plays (Remotion `<Freeze>` or a still).
- **PLAY beats with movie audio** as the default between pauses (today they're rare).
- **SFX layer**: a spacebar-style click on every pause and resume, a whoosh/pop on number pops, and a short "bass drop" or record-scratch under the biggest punch. Generate these ourselves (synthesised) so there are no licensing problems.
- Meme cut-ins ~1 s each, about every 6–8 s, synced to a word. The user supplies the files.
- Captions only during VO. No captions over movie dialogue (or use subtle subtitles).

## 7. Script file format (v2)

Each `episodes/NN-*/script.md`:
1. **Header:** movie, exact scene, approximate timestamps, title bar, upload title (question + 💀 style), coverage check (not covered by either reference channel).
2. **Numbers** (off-screen, checked), kept to 2–3 numbers total.
3. **Beat table:** `# | PLAY/PAUSE | movie timestamp or what's on screen | VO line with [tags] | overlay/SFX | est. seconds`
4. **Timing check:** total movie seconds vs. VO seconds (VO ≈ words ÷ 2.6 per second), and the word count.
5. **VO text block**: exactly what goes to ElevenLabs, with tags, beats separated by `[long pause]`.
