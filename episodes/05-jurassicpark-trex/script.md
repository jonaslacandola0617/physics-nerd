## Numbers (checked)
- **86% leg muscle:** Hutchinson & Garcia, *Nature* 2002. To run fast (~20 m/s, i.e. ~72 km/h, car speed), a ~6 t T. rex would need ~43% of its body mass as extensor muscle **per leg**, so ~86% for both. A chicken has ~10% per leg, so **~20%** for both.
- **~28 km/h:** Sellers et al., *PeerJ* 2017. The modelled safe top speed is just under 28 km/h (a fast walk). At running speeds, the leg-bone stresses would likely break the bones.
- The Jeep (a Wrangler) can comfortably do 55+ km/h on that road, which is "double".
- The convex side mirror really does make objects look smaller and farther away, so the sticker is true.

## VO text (exactly what would go to ElevenLabs)

[incredulous] Wait. Why is the Jeep losing to a T. rex?
[long pause]
Okay. This thing weighs around six tons. Scientists worked out that for something this heavy to run as fast as a car, about eighty-six percent of its body would have to be leg muscle. A chicken is around twenty.
[short pause]
[deadpan] So it's basically just legs... with a head.
[long pause]
Newer studies put its real top speed at about twenty-eight kilometers an hour. Any faster, and its own leg bones could snap.
[short pause]
[sarcastic] And that Jeep can do double that. They're being chased by a guy on a bicycle.
[long pause]
Okay, credit where it's due. That mirror is convex, so things really are closer than they appear.
[long pause]
[deadpan] Anyway, Muldoon... it wasn't chasing you. It was escorting you. Subscribe so I can afford a car faster than a dinosaur.
