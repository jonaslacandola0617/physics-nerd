## EP26 Speed: the freeway gap
- ✅ 70 mph = 103 ft/s, so 50 ft takes ~0.5 s and gravity drops the bus ~4 ft (checked). ✅ A loaded city bus weighs ~15 tons. ✅ The front wheels leave first while the rear is still supported, so the bus pitches nose-down: that's correct tipping physics.
- ⚠️ The fun fact: the real stunt bus did go off a hidden ramp, but there was **no gap**. The freeway gap was added digitally, and the bus flew much farther than planned. Suggested line: *"Fun fact: the real stunt bus flew off a hidden ramp, and the gap itself was added in post. Movie magic beats gravity every time."* ("Stripped out the seats" isn't something I can confirm; drop it.)
- ❓ Check that the bus visibly tilts nose-up in the shot (beat 2 depends on it).

## EP27 Avengers: Age of Ultron: lifting Sokovia
- ✅ Rock is strong in compression and weak in tension (tensile strength is roughly a tenth of compressive or less). That point is right.
- ❓ **"Lift from the middle / one finger":** in the film, Ultron's lifting machines may be spread across the underside rather than in one central spot. If several thrusters are visible, change *"from the middle"* to *"from a handful of points"* and *"one finger"* to *"a few fingers"*. The point (rock between the lift points cracks under its own weight) still holds.
- ⚠️ "Two kilometers wide · over a billion tons" depends on the size of the chunk. A 2 km × 200 m slab is ~1.7 billion tons, but if the chunk is closer to 1 km it's a few hundred million. Safer: *"hundreds of millions of tons of city and bedrock"*. Pop-up: *"100s of millions of tons"*.
- ✅ "Read the entire internet" is fine as a joke.

## EP28 Gravity: Clooney lets go
- ✅ Once they're stopped relative to the station, nothing pulls Kowalski away. In orbit, a gentle tug brings him back. This is a widely cited physics mistake in the film, and the script explains it correctly.
- Fine to keep the actor names. The characters are Ryan Stone and Matt Kowalski, if you'd rather use those.

## EP29 F9: the Fiero in space
- ✅ Orbit needs ~17,500 mph sideways. ✅ Orbital rockets are ~85–90% propellant. ✅ 14.7 psi over a windshield-plus-doors area is tens of thousands of pounds (a windshield alone is ~17 tons of outward force).
- ❓ Check the footage for "smashes through the satellite" and the diving suits.

## EP30 The Dark Knight: catching Rachel
- ✅ Terminal velocity ~120 mph. ✅ 40 mph to 0 in ~1 ft ≈ 54 G (checked: v²/2d).
- ❓ Is it a taxi? Does Batman land underneath? Is a driver visible ("staring up through the caved-in ceiling")? If there's no driver in the shot, change the cue to Batman and Rachel on the roof.

## EP31 Armageddon: the nuke
- ✅ NASA does use Armageddon in management training to spot errors (reportedly 168). ⚠️ **"…and this ending is number one"** isn't sourced. Cut it, or say *"…and this ending is the biggest one."*
- ⚠️ Texas is ~770–800 miles wide, so *"roughly eight hundred miles wide"* is closer than seven hundred. Pop-up: *"~800 Miles"*. (The script already has the asteroid "the size of Texas", which is the film's own line.)
- ✅ Drilling 800 ft is ~0.04% of the way to the centre, so "barely scratching the paint" is fair. ✅ University of Leicester students (2012): ~800 trillion terajoules, about a billion times the Tsar Bomba (checked: 8×10²⁶ J vs 4.2×10¹⁷ J).

## EP32 MI: Ghost Protocol: the Burj climb
- ✅ Cruise really did the climb on hidden cables. ✅ Stanford (Hawkes et al., 2015) built gecko-inspired pads that held a person on glass. ✅ Dust ruins dry adhesion, and these pads hold in shear (pulling down), not peel.
- ⚠️ The pads use microscopic **wedges** (gecko-*inspired*), not hairs. Suggested wording: *"…climbing paddles covered in microscopic wedges, like a gecko's foot…"*
- ❓ Beat 4's "sandstorm rolling toward Dubai" comes from a **later** scene (the chase). It's fine to use as a cutaway, but only if it's in the trimmed footage.
- ❓ Beat 6, "catches a ledge with bare fingertips": after the glove fails, he swings back on the rope and gets caught at the window. Check the footage and match the cue.

## EP33 Titanic: the door
- ✅ Room isn't the problem; buoyancy is. ✅ The water was about 28°F (−2°C). ✅ The panel tips when Jack tries to climb on.
- ⚠️ *"An object only floats if it pushes aside more weight in water than the weight sitting on top of it"* is slightly off, because the panel's own weight counts too. Suggested: *"A floating board can only hold up as much weight as the water it pushes aside, minus its own."* Or keep it simple: *"…pushes aside more water than the weight of everything on it."*
- ⚠️ **The life-jacket fix is from MythBusters (2012).** James Cameron's 2023 test was inconclusive ("maybe"). Suggested closer: *"MythBusters showed the only way Jack survives is tying Rose's life jacket under the panel for extra lift. So yeah: Rose wasn't selfish. Archimedes just ruined the romance."*
- "Thirty years" works (1997 → 2026 is 29).

## EP34 X-Men: Days of Future Past: Quicksilver
- ✅ Handgun bullets ~550–800 mph, so "around seven hundred" is fine. ✅ For bullets to look frozen, he has to be moving thousands of mph. ✅ F = ma whiplash is fine as hedged.
- The pop-up "Mach 10+" and the line "Mach ten" are estimates. They're fine as a joke because the VO says "thousands of miles an hour", which is safe.
- ❓ "Before he even tastes the soup": check that he tastes something in the scene.

## EP35 Die Hard 2: the jet-fuel trail
- ✅ Jet A flash point ≥ 100°F (38°C). Gasoline's is about −45°F. ✅ A lighter dropped into cold kerosene goes out. ✅ Flame spreads across a pool at only a few mph.
- ⚠️ **"The bad guys fly straight to the Bahamas"**: they were escaping to Val Verde (the fictional country). Suggested: *"In real life, the bad guys fly off to Val Verde while McClane stands on the runway wondering why his lighter won't work."*
- The 747 at takeoff is ~150–180 mph, so the "150+ mph" pop-up is fine.
