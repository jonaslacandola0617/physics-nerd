## EP11 The Avengers: Iron Man and the nuke (⚠️ 1 fix, 1 overlap)
- ✅ Tomahawk-class cruise missile ≈ 1,300–1,600 kg (~3,000 lb), cruises at ~550 mph.
- ⚠️ The Hulk catch. Hulk leaps across and catches Tony, then slides down the building, so the stop isn't a dead "split second". Defensible version: Tony is falling at roughly terminal velocity (~120 mph). Stopping that inside the length of Hulk's arms (~1 m) is still ~150 G. Suggested line: *"Catching a guy falling at over a hundred miles an hour, inside the length of your arms, still hits him with a hundred-plus Gs."*
- ⚠️ Overlap. EP06 already uses the "rigid suit plus sudden stop" punchline, so this beat repeats it. Keep it short, or lean on the "woke up because Hulk yelled" joke.
- ❓ Check the footage: what Tony grips (the tail or the body), and whether there's a visible exhaust near his hands.

## EP12 The Martian: the glove thruster (✅ mostly, 1 wording fix)
- ✅ NASA EMU ≈ 4.3 psi (29.6 kPa) pure O₂. A ~1 cm² hole gives ~4 N of thrust. Real.
- ✅ Off-centre thrust means torque and tumbling. Good, and it ties to Watney's own "Iron Man" line.
- ⚠️ The closer "a bold way to skip a rescue mission" doesn't land, because this *is* the rescue. Suggested line: *"Still, venting your own oxygen to go faster is the most Mark Watney plan possible."*
- Optional, stronger: all the gas in the suit and its tank (~0.5 kg) can only add ~1–2 m/s, about a jogging pace.
- ❓ Check the footage: does he actually spin before Lewis grabs him? The "to be fair" beat depends on it.

## EP13 Mission: Impossible – Rogue Nation: the plane hang (⚠️ 2 fixes)
- ✅ Cruise did 8 takes, held by a harness and cable through the door, wearing full-eye (scleral) contact lenses.
- ⚠️ The A400M lifts off at ~110–135 knots (~125–155 mph). "Around a hundred and fifty miles an hour" is OK, but 150 mph is a **Category Four** hurricane (Cat 5 starts at 157 mph). Change to "Category Four".
- ⚠️ "Armored contact lenses" → "special contact lenses". The stunt was filmed in 2014, so either use "twenty-fourteen" or say "hasn't slept since".
- ⚠️ "Pebble or bug … like a paintball": a pebble at 150 mph (~11 J) ≈ a paintball ✅; a bug is much weaker. Say "one tiny pebble".
- ❓ Check the footage: the suit, bare fingers on the door rim, and the hair.

## EP14 Infinity War: Thor and the star (⚠️ the physics needs reworking)
- ✅ Neutron star: 1.4–2 solar masses, ~12–15 miles across ("the size of a city"), surface gravity ~10¹¹ g.
- ⚠️ "Billions of times Earth's gravity" depends on distance: ~2 billion g at 100 km, ~20 million g at 1,000 km. Say *"millions of times stronger than Earth's"* to be safe.
- ⚠️ "Flatten into a pancake" is the wrong effect. A neutron star's gravity **stretches** you (tidal force, about 75 G across a 2 m body at 1,000 km, about 75,000 G at 100 km). Suggested line: *"…that gravity would stretch him, the dwarf, and the whole forge into spaghetti."* This is also a better gag.
- ❓ Check the subtitles: does the film actually say "neutron star"? Thor tells Rocket the forge "harnesses the power of a neutron star", but confirm before voicing.
- Note: the title repeats EP03's "Wrong For The WRONG Reason". Pick a different one.

## EP15 Godzilla (2014): walking through San Francisco (⚠️ 1 number fix)
- ✅ 355 ft (108 m), 90,000 short tons (official MonsterVerse figures) ≈ a Nimitz-class carrier.
- ✅ Square-cube law: weight ∝ L³, bone strength ∝ L².
- ✅ Ground pressure: 90,000 t on one ~200 m² foot ≈ 4 MPa, ~10× what ordinary soil supports.
- ⚠️ "Pump blood thirty stories up": his head is only ~40–50 m above his heart, so it's **about fifteen stories**. That still means ~3,500 mmHg, more than ten times a giraffe's blood pressure. Suggested line: *"…his heart would have to push blood fifteen stories straight up — more than ten times a giraffe's blood pressure."*
- ❓ Check the footage: use downtown San Francisco shots (Chinatown and the final fight), not Hawaii or Las Vegas.
