## EP06 Iron Man — ⚠️ one real fix
- ✅ Math: 45 m/s stopped in 1 m → ~103 G. Mark III is gold-titanium.
- ⚠️ "Over a hundred Gs… kills him" is overstated. People have survived 100+ G spikes (IndyCar driver Kenny Bräck, ~214 G in 2003), but only inside a car built to crumple, with restraints. That actually makes the point *better*: the suit has no crumple zone. Suggested line: *"Race drivers have survived crashes like that, but only because the car crumpled around them. A rigid metal suit doesn't crumple."*
- ⚠️ "Organs keep moving at a hundred miles an hour into the inside of his helmet" is loose physics. Better: *"His brain still slams into the inside of his skull at a hundred miles an hour."*
- ❓ Check the footage: is he knocked out of the sky by the tank, and is the fall high enough for ~100 mph? (~100 mph free fall needs ~100 m, and he's flying, so it's plausible.) Does he make a crater?

## EP07 Top Gun: Maverick — ⚠️ two fixes
- ✅ Mach 10 ≈ 6,600 mph at altitude (speed of sound ~660 mph). ~3× a rifle bullet (~2,000–2,700 mph). Bill Weaver, SR-71, Mach 3.18, 78,800 ft, Jan 1966.
- ⚠️ "Doctors basically called it a miracle" has no source. Use the true, stronger fact instead: **his crewmate, Jim Zwayer, did not survive.**
- ⚠️ "Ten times the force" is only true at the same altitude (wind blast ∝ air density × v²). Say *"roughly ten times the wind blast, at the same altitude."* "Vaporize him instantly" is hyperbole; "tear him apart" is defensible.
- ⚠️ Contradiction: beat 7 says he wears a full pressure suit, and beat 8 says "nothing but a dusty flight suit". Pick one (the movie shows a pressure suit).
- ❓ "A hundred thousand feet": I don't think the movie states the altitude. Say "up that high" unless the footage shows it. The ejection itself is never shown; the movie cuts from the breakup to the diner, so the hook should say "survived *that*".

## EP08 The Dark Knight — ⚠️ the core claim is shaky
- ✅ Empty tractor-trailer ≈ 15–17 short tons. Real flip on LaSalle Street, Chicago, using a nitrogen-powered ram, no CGI.
- ⚠️ "Modern streetlights are literally engineered to fail / breakaway bases" is mainly a **highway** rule (roadside clear zones). Downtown sidewalk poles usually *aren't* breakaway, because a falling pole would hit pedestrians. The joke should rest on something that's true either way: *"A streetlight is held down by a few anchor bolts rated for wind, not for stopping fifteen tons. They'd snap long before the truck flipped."*
- ⚠️ "Stronger than suspension bridges" is fine as a closing joke but it's wild. "Stronger than the truck" is enough.
- ❓ Check the footage: does Batman wrap the cables around the lampposts, or does he anchor them to the truck and weave the Batpod around the posts? The lines need to match exactly what's shown.

## EP09 John Wick — ✅ mostly fine, name the movie
- ✅ 9mm ≈ 450–500 J, 5.56 ≈ 1,700+ J, NIJ backface limit 44 mm, Garrison Bespoke makes real ballistic suits, a hit "like a baseball bat" (common comparison).
- ⚠️ "Energy doesn't magically disappear. That force still has to go somewhere" mixes energy and force. Say *"that impact still has to go somewhere."*

- ❓ Which movie? The jacket-as-shield move is mostly in **Chapter 4**; the tailor fitting is in **Chapter 2**. Pick one and check "doesn't even flinch" and "ten or fifteen rounds" against it.
