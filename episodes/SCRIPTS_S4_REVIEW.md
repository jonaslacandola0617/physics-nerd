## EP16 Furious 7: skyscraper jump
- ✅ 100 mph = 147 ft/s, so 150 ft takes ~1.0 s, and gravity drops the car 16–17 ft. ✅ The Lykan HyperSport cost ~$3.4 M ("three-million-dollar").
- ⚠️ 16 ft is about one and a half floors, not two. Change "smashing two stories lower" to "**a floor and a half lower**".
- ❓ The 150 ft gap, the ~60 ft room, and "no brakes" (is that said or shown?) all need checking against the footage.
- ⚠️ "thick laminated safety glass": high-rise facades are usually tempered or laminated insulated glass. Say "**thick tempered safety glass**". The point stands either way.

## EP17 Iron Man 2: suitcase suit
- ⚠️ In the Monaco scene it's **Pepper** who throws the case to Tony. Happy drives the Rolls into Whiplash. Rewrite the hook and closer around Pepper. Suggested hook: *"Pepper Potts just tossed a two-hundred-pound metal suit out of a Rolls-Royce like it's a weekend carry-on."* Closer: *"Forget the suitcase engineering. Someone needs to test Pepper Potts for Super Soldier Serum immediately."*
- ❓ "Pepper kisses the briefcase": I don't remember this happening. Check before you write it into the beat.
- ✅ A full metal suit at 150–250 lb is a reasonable estimate. The paper-thin plates argument is fine.

## EP18 Inception: the falling van
- ⚠️ **"The heavy engine makes it nose-dive" is wrong physics.** In free fall, gravity pulls every part of the van equally, so a heavy nose doesn't make it tip. A van only rotates if it left the bridge already spinning.
- ⚠️ **The hook attacks the one thing Nolan got right.** Because the van is in free fall, everyone inside is weightless. That's exactly why the hotel level loses gravity, which is real physics. That should be the "to be fair" beat.
- ✅ A 55 ft drop means hitting the water at ~40 mph (checked).
- ⚠️ "Hits like solid concrete" overstates it. At 40 mph water hits hard, about like a 40 mph car crash, but not like concrete.
- Suggested structure:
  1. Hook: *"Christopher Nolan actually got the zero gravity right here — and then dropped a van off a bridge at forty miles an hour onto a sleeping crew."*
  2. To be fair: the van is in free fall, so everyone inside floats. That's why the hotel loses gravity, and it's genuinely correct.
  3. The catch: a 55 ft drop is a 40 mph impact. Water at 40 mph is like a 40 mph crash, with everyone asleep and unbraced.
  4. Closer: keep "half the squad is waking up in an ambulance."

## EP19 Pacific Rim: cargo ship bat
- ✅ A ship lifted by one end acts as a cantilever, and the hull fails in bending. Ships are built to be supported along the bottom.
- ⚠️ "Real freighters break in half just from hitting a bad ocean wave" is overstated. Say *"**real freighters have snapped in half in heavy seas**"* (MOL Comfort, 2013, is a real example).
- ❓ Check the ship's length on screen ("three-hundred-foot").

## EP20 Interstellar: Miller's wave
- ✅ Gravity 130% of Earth's. ✅ Kip Thorne won the 2017 Nobel Prize and modeled the waves as a tidal effect. ✅ The pressure at the base of a 4,000 ft wave is about 2,300 psi (checked).
- ⚠️ **But the Ranger isn't at the base.** It rides up the face near the surface, where the pressure is close to normal. "It would crush it" doesn't follow.
- Fix, keeping your 2,000+ psi line: *"If that wave had rolled over the Ranger instead of lifting it, the ship would be sitting under four thousand feet of water — over two thousand pounds per square inch. Spaceships are built to hold air in, not keep the ocean out."* Then closer: *"And somehow the only problem is flooded engines?"*

## EP21 Indiana Jones 4: fridge
- ✅ Leading with the radiation point and then switching to the G-forces is a good structure. The "lead-lined" label is shown in the film.
- ⚠️ "Half a mile through the air in two seconds" and "three-hundred-pound" are unsourced. Say *"the blast wave launches a metal box hundreds of feet through the air"*. "Hundreds of G's" is fine as a hedged estimate.
- ❓ Check whether the fridge bounces down a hill or tumbles across flat desert.

## EP22 The Matrix: bullet dodge
- ✅ .50 AE at ~1,400–1,500 ft/s means 25 ft takes ~17 ms (checked). ✅ Reaction time ~200 ms. ✅ Once your center of mass is behind your heels, you fall.
- ⚠️ "The fastest human reaction time on Earth is around two hundred milliseconds": 200 ms is *typical*, and elite sprinters react in ~120–150 ms. Say *"a normal human reaction time is around two hundred milliseconds. That bullet is ten times faster."*
- ❓ Check that it's a Desert Eagle in that shot.

## EP23 Spider-Man: Homecoming ferry
- ⚠️ "Three thousand tons" is the ferry's **gross tonnage**, which measures volume, not weight. Say *"**thousands of tons**"* and *"**over a thousand tons on each side**"*. The joke works just as well.
- ✅ The webs fail and Iron Man fixes it, so "partial credit" is right.
- ⚠️ "Holding both halves together with his bare arms": he's holding web lines, so "**with nothing but webs and his arms**".

## EP24 Star Wars: asteroid field
- ✅ C-3PO: "approximately 3,720 to 1". ✅ About 600,000 miles between asteroids, more than twice the Earth–Moon distance (NASA's commonly quoted figure). ✅ Over a dozen probes have crossed the belt.
- ⚠️ "Off by about a billion" has no source. Say *"**Real astronomy says those odds are way too pessimistic.**"* or *"**off by a few million percent**"*.
- ⚠️ "Without even bothering to steer" → "**without dodging a single rock**". Probes do steer; they just don't need to dodge anything.

## EP25 Fast & Furious 6: runway
- ✅ About 13 minutes of screen time. The ~18–29 mile runway estimates are widely published. ✅ The longest paved runway is Qamdo Bamda at ~3.4 mi. ✅ An An-124 weighs up to ~400 t.
- ❓ It's more than two cars harpooning the plane, so say "**a few regular cars**". Check against the footage.
