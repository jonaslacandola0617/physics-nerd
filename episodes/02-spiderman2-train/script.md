## Fact-check (Claude)
- 200 t at 70 mph (31.3 m/s), stopped in ~30 s: a ≈ 1.04 m/s², so F ≈ 208 kN ≈ **21 tonnes-force**. "Roughly twenty tons" ✅
- 10 t per arm vs. the ~0.5 t deadlift record, so ×20. "Twenty world records per shoulder" ✅ (loose but fair)
- Spider silk ~1–1.5 GPa, so ~1 cm² carries ~10 t. "Could handle the tension" ✅
- Brick anchors failing first is plausible (masonry pull-out loads are far below 10 t per anchor). ✅

## Clean VO
```text
Bro, did he really just try to stop a runaway subway train... with his shoes?

Okay, fine, he switches to webs—much better. Except a packed subway train weighs around two hundred tons, and it's moving at seventy miles an hour. To bring all that mass to a dead stop in about thirty seconds takes roughly twenty tons of braking force. Split that down the middle, and that's ten tons pulling on each arm.

The world-record deadlift is half a ton using both arms. Peter is basically holding twenty world records per shoulder at the exact same time.

To be fair, real spider silk scaled up to that thickness actually could handle the tension. The webs aren't the problem here.

The problem is Peter's shoulders—and those brick walls—should've exploded before the train even slowed down. Honestly, passing out right after is the most realistic thing he does all movie.
```
