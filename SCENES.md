# Scene Shortlist

Ranked by: an iconic scene, **one** clean calculation, a real-world comparison, and an easy closing joke. Scenes the reference channels already covered (Pacific Rim helicopters, the Avengers Tower fall) are left out.

Timestamps are **approximate** (they vary by release and streaming cut). I'll pin them down exactly once you drop the file in `videos/`.

---

### 1. Interstellar — "No time for caution" docking spin  ⭐ top pick
- **Scene:** the Endurance is spinning at about 67–68 RPM, and Cooper matches the spin to dock. (~2:15:00)
- **Hook:** "Cooper just docked with a spaceship spinning more than once a second… and somehow nobody passed out."
- **Math:** 68 RPM ≈ 7.1 rad/s. Centripetal acceleration is ω²r, which is about 50 m/s² (≈5 g) for every metre you sit off the spin axis. A couple of metres off-axis puts you past what fighter pilots train for, and that's without the tumbling.
- **Comparison:** a fighter-pilot centrifuge, or a washing machine spin cycle.
- **Closer:** "Respect to whoever designed that docking clamp."

### 2. Spider-Man 2 — stopping the train
- **Scene:** Spidey webs the buildings and stops the runaway elevated train. (~1:30:00)
- **Hook:** "Spider-Man is trying to stop a train… with his arms."
- **Math:** a loaded subway car weighs about 40 t, and there are several cars. Going from ~80 km/h to a stop in a few hundred metres means a stopping force in the hundreds of kN, roughly tens of tons pulling through each shoulder.
- **Comparison:** the record deadlift (~500 kg).
- **Closer:** "The real superpower is his shoulder joints."

### 3. Captain America: Civil War — holding the helicopter
- **Scene:** Cap grabs the taking-off helicopter with one hand, the other hand on the building edge. (~1:20:00)
- **Hook:** "Cap is out-pulling a helicopter. With one bicep."
- **Math:** the rotor produces thrust of at least the aircraft's weight (several tons) to climb. All of it goes through one arm and one grip.
- **Comparison:** the strongest grip ever measured is around 150 kg.
- **Closer:** "Peak human, apparently, means peak hydraulic press."

### 4. Top Gun: Maverick — Mach 10 Darkstar
- **Scene:** Maverick pushes past Mach 10, the jet breaks apart, and he ejects. (~0:05:00, opening)
- **Hook:** "He ejected at Mach 10. Let's talk about that."
- **Math:** Mach 10 at altitude is about 3 km/s. Dynamic pressure and heating at that speed would make the ejection itself the deadliest part. Real ejections above ~Mach 3 are rarely survivable.
- **Comparison:** the SR-71 at Mach 3.2, whose skin reached ~300 °C.
- **Closer:** "Then he walks into a diner. Sure."

### 5. Iron Man 3 — "Barrel of monkeys"
- **Scene:** 13 people fall from Air Force One, and Iron Man links them up midair. (~1:25:00)
- **Hook:** "Thirteen people at terminal velocity. One suit."
- **Math:** each person falls at ~55 m/s. Stopping a 1,000 kg human chain in ~1 s takes ~55 kN, about 5.6 g on everyone, and that's on top of their arms holding the chain together.
- **Comparison:** a hard parachute opening is ~3–5 g.
- **Closer:** "The real hero is everyone's rotator cuff."

### 6. The Martian — the "Iron Man" maneuver
- **Scene:** Watney punctures his glove and uses the escaping air as a thruster. (~2:05:00)
- **Hook:** "He used a hole in his glove as a rocket. Does that work?"
- **Math:** suit pressure is ~30 kPa, and the leak velocity and thrust come out at single-digit newtons. Then you weigh it against how much air he loses.
- **Comparison:** the push of a desk fan.
- **Closer:** an honest "surprisingly… not crazy" verdict. (Mixing in a "they got it right" video builds trust.)

### 7. Godzilla (2014) — can he even stand?
- **Scene:** Godzilla's first full reveal at the airport. (~1:00:00)
- **Hook:** "Godzilla is basically a skyscraper with legs."
- **Math:** the square-cube law. Scale a 2 m lizard up ~50× and its weight goes up 125,000×, but the leg cross-section only goes up 2,500×. Then compare the pressure on each foot to what the ground can take.
- **Comparison:** an elephant's leg bones, scaled up.
- **Closer:** "Every footstep is a free sinkhole."

### 8. Armageddon — the one-nuke split
- **Scene:** the bomb is drilled 800 ft in, detonates, and the asteroid splits in two. (~2:15:00)
- **Hook:** "One nuke. An asteroid the size of Texas."
- **Math:** the energy needed to split it and push the halves apart vs. the largest bomb ever detonated (Tsar Bomba, 50 Mt).
- **Comparison:** Tsar Bomba.
- **Closer:** "Bruce Willis needed a bigger drill."
